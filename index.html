My Vision

Building at the speed of thought

We’re living through a shift in how software gets made. With AI and vibe coding, the distance between an idea and a working product has shrunk from months to hours. I see this as a reshaping of what it means to be a builder rather than a threat to it.

Vibe coding is a new way to create

Vibe coding means describing what you want in plain language, guiding the AI, and iterating until it feels right. It lowers the barrier to entry, so designers, students, founders and domain experts can now turn ideas into real products without years of syntax drilled into their heads. I embrace it because it lets me spend less time on boilerplate and more time on the questions that matter: What problem am I solving? Who is it for? Does it feel right to use?

Speed needs judgment

AI can write code quickly, but it can’t take responsibility for it. The real skill is in knowing what to build, reviewing what’s generated, catching what’s fragile, and understanding the architecture well enough to fix things when they break. I treat AI as a fast, tireless collaborator, and I stay the one making the decisions. Fundamentals still matter. They’re what let me steer the tools instead of being steered by them.

The AI boom is a moment of opportunity

We’re at the start of a wave comparable to the internet or mobile. Products that once needed whole teams can now be prototyped by one person with curiosity and good taste. I want to be on the building side of this boom: learning continuously, shipping often, and turning new capabilities into things people actually use.

What I believe going forward

  • Human + AI beats either alone. The best work comes from creativity and context paired with machine speed.
  • Learning never stops. Tools change every few months, so adaptability is the real career skill.
  • Build responsibly. Think about privacy, bias, security and the people who will use what we make.
  • Craft still matters. Easy creation doesn’t excuse careless work. Good design, clean code and thoughtful details set great products apart.
  • Ship and iterate. Done and improving beats perfect and unreleased.

Where I’m headed

My goal is to use these tools to build useful, human-centered products, keep growing as an engineer, and help shape a future where technology makes more people capable of creating. The tools will keep evolving, and I plan to evolve with them.
